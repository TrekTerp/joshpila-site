Include PDO in eligibility
Off by default: PDO (Property Damage Only) crashes are still counted, mapped, and costed, but don't by themselves count toward Min Crashes/KSI or Recurrence for a candidate corridor. Once a corridor qualifies on other grounds, its PDO crashes still contribute to displayed totals and cost/score.
Crash-Focused Trimming
Gap acceptance 150 ft
Edge buffer 50 ft
Fill threshold 75%
Within a physically-connected road, crash-bearing sections farther apart than Gap acceptance become separate candidate corridors. Edge buffer extends each candidate slightly past its outermost crash. Fill threshold is a backstop for physically-continuous roads (not the same as a real break, like Bancroft Way's — see below): after edge-trimming, if the selected share of the remaining span meets this percentage, the internal gaps fill in and the whole span becomes one corridor — avoids over-fragmenting a busy jurisdiction-wide street into many small pieces just because a few individual blocks fell under Gap acceptance. These define initial screening geometry — review corridor limits before publication, same as any other candidate boundary this tool proposes.

Network distance follows the OSM road network (freeways excluded) and mapped paths; points more than ~800 ft from any road or path are left out. Bike Facilities
Loads automatically from OpenStreetMap after the road network.
Also use OSM facilities
Save layer:
On-road facilities only (Classes II–IV): OpenStreetMap tags mapped to CA classes — Class II from bike lanes, Class IV from cycle tracks or physically separated lanes, Class III from sharrows, bicycle streets, and signed bike routes. A separately mapped path running beside a road counts as a separated facility on that road (verify: Class IV or a Class I sidepath). Other off-street paths are trails — they aren't corridor facilities, but their road crossings are found under Trail Crossings. This is an inference from crowdsourced tags; an agency layer (upload or layer URL, with a class field; Class I lines are ignored) is more reliable and turns OSM off by default. With "Set class on map" on, click a road to set its class (or "no bikeway") for that block or the whole street; edits override every source and are saved in this browser for this jurisdiction. Lines are matched to roads that run parallel within ~80 ft. Corridors show 🚲 with the most-separated class, or "🚲 none" when a corridor with bike crashes has no facility matched; exports add coverage % by class and bike crash / bike KSI counts. "Save layer" writes the bikeways in use, with your edits, as GeoJSON lines with a class field that uploads back in.
Trail Crossings
Found automatically from OpenStreetMap trails after the road network.
Crash buffers ft
Save layer:
A trail crossing is where an off-street path (OpenStreetMap cycleway, or a path or footway designated for bikes — not a sidepath, bridge, or tunnel) meets a public road at a shared point and continues on both sides. Service roads, parking aisles, and freeways are excluded; crossings within ~65 ft of each other (e.g. both halves of a divided road) count once. Type comes from an OSM crossing node at the spot, the crossing's own tags, or a traffic signal within ~50 ft: marked uncontrolled, flashing beacon (e.g. RRFB), signalized (signal or PHB), unmarked, or type not mapped. Each counts toward the road segment it crosses. Corridors show ⛙ with a count (red outline when one is marked and uncontrolled); exports add counts by type and the ped and bike crashes within the crash buffers. An agency inventory (points, with a type field) replaces the detected crossings. With "Edit on map" on, click empty map to add a crossing, or click a crossing to retype, rename, move, or delete it; edits are saved in this browser for this jurisdiction.

Include private schools
Save layer:
Statewide layer: active public schools and private schools from the CA Department of Education, via the CA state GIS service — one point per school address, not the campus outline. Distances are measured from the school point and any access points you add (driveways, gates). Row proximity (½ mile default — a screening distance, not a mapped regulatory school zone) decides which corridors and intersections are tagged; crash buffers (ped ½ mi / bike 1 mi default) decide which ped and bike crashes count as near a school, in exports and the crash CSV for Cross-Factor Diagnosis. Each school keeps only its name, type (e.g. Elementary, High, Private), and grades. Corridors and intersections within range show 🏫 with a count, and exports add school counts, names, and ped/bike crashes near a school. With "Edit on map" on, click empty map to add a school, or click a school to rename, retype, move, delete it, or add access points; edits are saved in this browser for this jurisdiction. "Save layer" writes the schools in use, with your edits, in a format that uploads back in.
